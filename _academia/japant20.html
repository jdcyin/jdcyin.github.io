The goal

Health coverage for everyone by 2030

Universal health coverage is a priority for the G20, the World Health Organization and the UN. On current trajectories, four areas are unlikely to progress fast enough to meet the target.

The goal

Written for the G20

The brief was prepared by a working group convened by Japan, as host of the 2019 Think 20 summit, and published in The BMJ ahead of the G20 leaders' meeting in Osaka.

1 · Leaving no one behind

Primary care for ageing populations

Strong primary care reduces inequity. As populations age, it needs to shift towards the sustained care that non-communicable diseases require.

1 · Leaving no one behind

258 million people on the move

In 2017 there were 258 million international migrants, 3.4% of the world's population. Their health is a unique and so far neglected challenge for universal health coverage.

1 · Leaving no one behind

Most of them move for work

Work drives 64% of migration: 164 million migrant workers. Many G20 members are major destinations, so they are well placed to lead on migrant health and shared migration data.

1 · Leaving no one behind

Health systems run on migrants too

Health systems in many countries rely on foreign-trained doctors, often recruited from lower income countries.

1 · Leaving no one behind

And on foreign-trained nurses

Their migration means source countries lose their investment in training. The G20 should systematically measure the mobility of the health workforce.

2 · Financing

Demand is outpacing the money

Health systems face rising demand at a time when many countries are experiencing fiscal stagnation. Mobilising domestic resources is crucial, and so is fiscal discipline.

2 · Financing

Finance and health, working together

Reliable financing needs finance and health ministries in deliberate dialogue. Japan offers one model.

Making every resource count

Affordable, good-quality medicines matter, especially antimicrobials, where misuse drives resistance. UHC and resistance plans converge on six policies that governments can act on.

3 · Innovation

Harness digital health, but regulate it

Digital health could transform access to care. Governments need rules in place before it reaches a tipping point, and we proposed a G20 working group to set them.

4 · Cooperation

Monitor and learn together

Common monitoring and shared learning platforms let countries compare progress and learn what works, how and why.

Cooperation
1 Equity 2 Money 3 Tech 4 Aid
4 · Cooperation

Harmonise development aid

A typical recipient country faces dozens of donors, agencies and alliances with overlapping but competing demands. As the largest providers of development assistance, G20 members should coordinate, avoiding duplication and filling gaps.
